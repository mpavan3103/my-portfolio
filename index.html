About Me

I am M Pavan, hailing from Kottayam, Kerala, currently pursuing my first year of B.Tech in Computer Science and Engineering at Vellore Institute of Technology (VIT), Vellore. My academic specialisation focuses on Human-Computer Interaction (HCI), Ubiquitous Computing, and Artificial Intelligence — driven by the conviction that computing systems should adapt seamlessly to human cognitive habits rather than demanding fragmented attention.

My short-term goal is to master foundational algorithms, conduct undergraduate research in context-aware systems, and build robust software prototypes such as my commodity intelligence tool, PriceGuard AI. In the long term, I aspire to engineer calm computing environments and assistive interfaces that enhance human productivity at global technology leaders.

My unique talent lies in bridging software intelligence with human empathy — translating technical machine learning concepts into transparent, accessible user experiences. Through open-source software and privacy-conscious engineering, my goal is to contribute technology to society that empowers everyday people, respects user privacy, and reduces digital overload.

Beyond technology, I am deeply fascinated by astronomy — the profound scale of the cosmos puts engineering challenges into perspective and continually inspires me to think beyond immediate boundaries.

Projects Planned (Next 3 Years)

Proposed technical projects planned for development during 2026–2029 across HCI and ubiquitous computing.

Planned Project · 2027

Adaptive Smart Workspace

Problem Statement

Extended screen time leads to attentional fatigue, eye strain, and context fragmentation due to static, unvarying desk lighting and uncurated notification streams.

Proposed Approach

Build an interconnected desk daemon combining ambient lux sensors, acoustic monitoring, and a desktop background service that automatically adapts display color warmth, peripheral bias lighting, and notification strictness based on user workflow state.

Intended Outcome

An open-source hardware kit and cross-platform desktop utility that measurably reduces task-interruption frequency during intensive coding sessions.

Hardware reference: SparkFun Electronics (CC BY 2.0)

Planned Project · 2027–2028

User-Controlled Adaptive Notification System

Problem Statement

Modern operating systems treat every notification with identical urgency, breaking deep focus cycles and causing attention fragmentation across phones, smartwatches, and laptops.

Proposed Approach

Develop a client-side heuristic dispatch proxy that classifies incoming alerts by user-defined importance profiles (e.g., "Deep Problem Solving", "Lecture Mode", "Reachable") and batches non-critical pings into scheduled summaries.

Intended Outcome

A privacy-preserving browser extension and Android background service providing transparent user control without passing private alert bodies to external cloud servers.

Publications Planned (Next 3 Years)

Research Proposal Notice: These are proposed publication directions representing planned undergraduate research inquiries. They are not published papers, accepted manuscripts, or completed empirical studies.

Proposed Publication · Target 2027

Evaluating Cognitive Load and Interruption Costs in Activity-Aware Notification Orchestration

Research Question & Objective

To quantify the reduction in attentional resumption lag when notifications are deferred to natural task transition boundaries versus immediate delivery during multi-window coding workflows.

Theoretical Motivation

Grounded in attention economics and cognitive load theory, exploring how software interfaces can act as courteous mediators rather than attention adversaries.

Proposed Methodology

Within-subjects laboratory study with 25+ undergraduate programmers, measuring task completion times, typing resumption lag, and subjective NASA-TLX workload scores.

Target Venue & Roadmap

Targeting undergraduate HCI symposium tracks (e.g., student research competitions at ACM CHI / UIST regional chapters) in 2027.

Hardware reference: Japanwikisan (CC BY 4.0)

Proposed Publication · Target 2028

Transparent Privacy-Control Architectures for Ubiquitous Ambient Sensor Networks

Research Question & Objective

How can ambient computing environments communicate real-time sensing states to occupants through subtle peripheral cues, while offering frictionless, verifiable data revocation?

Theoretical Motivation

Smart buildings and shared student dormitories increasingly embed microphones and vision sensors without understandable user consent models. Investigating physical-digital privacy parity.

Proposed Methodology

Co-design participatory workshops followed by deployment of a physical sensor testbed equipped with mechanical privacy shutters and clear ambient status indicators.

Target Venue & Roadmap

Targeting ubiquitous computing and pervasive privacy workshops (e.g., ACM Pervasive / IMWUT student workshops) in 2028.
